# Energy-Efficient Roofing Options for Maryland Homeowners

Maryland summers are no joke. Baltimore hits an average of 31 days above 90°F each year, and the humid Mid-Atlantic air makes it feel worse. A standard dark asphalt shingle roof can reach surface temperatures of 150-170°F on a July afternoon — and that heat transfers straight into your attic, forcing your HVAC to work harder all day. Switching to an energy-efficient roofing system can cut cooling loads by 10-15% and reduce attic temperatures by 20-40°F. Here's what's available to Maryland homeowners, what it costs, and how to offset the expense.

## What Makes a Roof "Energy Efficient"?

Two properties matter: solar reflectance and thermal emittance. Solar reflectance (SRI) measures how much sunlight a surface reflects rather than absorbs. Thermal emittance measures how quickly the surface releases absorbed heat. The EPA's ENERGY STAR program requires roofing products to meet a minimum 0.25 initial solar reflectance for low-slope roofs and 0.25 (0.15 aged) for steep-slope products to earn certification.

Cool roofs score high on both metrics. A standard dark asphalt shingle has an SRI around 5-20. A light-colored reflective shingle from a product line like GAF's Timberline CS (Cooler Series) or CertainTeed's Landmark Solaris can hit SRI 25-35 — not as high as a commercial white membrane, but meaningful on a residential pitch.

Maryland's mixed climate (IECC Climate Zone 4, with portions of Western Maryland in Zone 5) means you also have to think about winter. A roof that reflects too much solar energy can marginally increase heating loads from November through March. The net annual savings typically still favor high-reflectance products in Maryland, but the math is closer than it is in Florida or Texas.

## Cool Roof Shingles: The Practical Choice for Most Maryland Homes

Reflective asphalt shingles are the easiest upgrade for homeowners replacing an existing roof. They install identically to standard shingles, cost $0.50-$1.50 more per square foot of material, and come in colors that look like conventional roofing — not the bright white most people picture when they hear "cool roof."

Products worth comparing:

- **GAF Timberline CS**: SRI 29-38 depending on color. Available through GAF Master Elite contractors. 25-year color warranty.
- **CertainTeed Landmark Solaris**: SRI 25-36. Comes in 15 color options, most in beige and gray tones common in Annapolis and Bethesda neighborhoods.
- **Owens Corning Duration Cool**: Uses Cool Roof Rating Council (CRRC)-rated granules. SRI varies by color (22-35). Eligible for ENERGY STAR labeling in lighter colorways.

For a 2,000 sq ft home in Columbia or Rockville, expect total installed cost of $9,500-$15,000 for cool asphalt shingles versus $8,500-$13,500 for standard shingles. The premium is real, but it's paid once — and a well-ventilated attic combined with a reflective shingle can shorten payback to 7-12 years depending on your current energy bills.

## Metal Roofing: Higher Upfront, Best Long-Term Performance

Standing-seam metal roofing — typically steel or aluminum with a factory-applied PVDF coating — outperforms reflective shingles on both SRI and longevity. A light-colored metal roof can achieve SRI 65-85 and last 40-60 years with minimal maintenance. That matters in Maryland, where nor'easters, ice dams along the I-270 corridor, and summer hail in Montgomery and Frederick counties put cumulative stress on roofing materials.

Metal also handles snow load differently than asphalt. It sheds ice and snow faster, reducing the risk of ice dams that cause ceiling leaks in older Maryland homes. If your home is in a neighborhood with mature tree canopy — think Roland Park in Baltimore or the Chevy Chase suburbs — metal's impact resistance (typically Class 4 hail rating) is worth the premium.

Installed cost for standing-seam metal on a 2,000 sq ft home in Maryland runs $18,000-$28,000. That's a significant gap over asphalt, but the 40+ year lifespan means one roof replacement instead of two or three over the same period.

Stone-coated steel panels (Decra, Gerard) offer a middle ground: they look like tile or shake, carry Class 4 impact ratings, and install for $14,000-$22,000 on a typical Maryland home.

## Maryland EmPOWER Program: What Homeowners Can Actually Claim

Maryland EmPOWER Maryland is the state's energy efficiency incentive program, administered through the utility companies (BGE, Pepco, Delmarva Power, SMECO, and others). It provides rebates and low-interest financing for home energy improvements — but the direct rebate for roofing itself is limited.

As of 2026, EmPOWER does not offer a standalone rebate specifically for cool roof shingles or metal roofing on existing residential homes. What it does offer:

- **Attic air sealing and insulation rebates**: Up to $1,500 through BGE's program when combined with a qualified energy audit. This is highly relevant to a roof replacement, because the right time to address attic insulation is while the roof deck is accessible.
- **Home Performance with ENERGY STAR (HPwES)**: A whole-home audit program that can unlock $1,500-$4,000 in stacked rebates across insulation, HVAC, and air sealing. A new reflective roof combined with upgraded attic insulation qualifies as a system-level improvement.
- **Financing at 0% APR**: EmPOWER offers unsecured energy efficiency loans through participating lenders at 0% interest for up to $10,000, repaid through your utility bill. This is genuinely useful for financing the attic work that accompanies a roof replacement.

The federal 25C energy-efficient home improvement tax credit (extended through 2032 under the Inflation Reduction Act) covers insulation and air sealing at 30% of cost, up to $1,200/year. It does not cover roofing materials directly — the old "metal roof credit" was not renewed for residential replacements.

Bottom line: plan the attic insulation and air sealing work alongside your roof replacement to capture the available incentives. Don't buy a new roof expecting a state check for the shingles themselves.

## Ventilation: The Factor Most Homeowners Miss

No cool roof product performs as advertised with inadequate attic ventilation. Maryland's 2021 Residential Building Code (based on IRC 2018 with state amendments) requires a minimum net free ventilation area of 1/150 of attic floor area, or 1/300 when half the ventilation area is in the upper portion of the attic (ridge vents, off-ridge vents, or gable vents in the upper third).

In practice, many Maryland homes built before 1990 — particularly row homes in Baltimore City and older subdivisions in Prince George's County — are significantly under-ventilated. A reflective shingle on a poorly ventilated attic will still see attic temperatures above 130°F because heat builds up with nowhere to go.

A proper ventilation upgrade — ridge vent plus soffit intake baffles — runs $800-$2,500 installed and should be included in any energy-efficiency-focused roof replacement.

## Is a Cool Roof Worth It in Maryland's Climate?

The honest answer: yes, for most homeowners, but the payback period is longer than in purely hot climates. The DOE's Oak Ridge National Laboratory estimates a 10-20% reduction in cooling energy for Maryland-climate homes with high-SRI roofs and proper ventilation. On a BGE or Pepco bill averaging $180-$240/month in summer, that's roughly $200-$500 in annual cooling savings — plus reduced wear on your HVAC equipment.

If you're already replacing an aging roof, the incremental cost to upgrade to cool shingles ($1,000-$2,000 on a typical job) pays back in 4-8 years. If you're considering metal specifically for energy reasons, the calculation is tighter — though the durability and hail resistance often close the gap for homes in Frederick, Hagerstown, or other areas with frequent severe weather.

The clearest case for energy-efficient roofing in Maryland: homes with older HVAC systems, poor attic insulation, or south- and west-facing roof planes with minimal shading.

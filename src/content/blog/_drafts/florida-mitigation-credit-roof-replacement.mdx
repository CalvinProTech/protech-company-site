# How a New Roof Earns You a Florida Wind Mitigation Credit

Replacing your roof in Florida is expensive — typically $9,000-$18,000 for a 1,500-2,500 sq ft home depending on materials and county. What most homeowners don't realize until after the job is done is that the right roof, properly documented, can reduce their annual insurance premium by 20-40%. That reduction comes from Florida's wind mitigation credit program, triggered by a single two-page form: the OIR-B1-1802.

Here's exactly how the form works, which roof features produce the largest credits, and how to move through the inspection timeline without losing money on delayed premium adjustments.

## What the OIR-B1-1802 Form Actually Is

The OIR-B1-1802 — formally titled the "Uniform Mitigation Verification Inspection Form" — was developed by the Florida Office of Insurance Regulation to standardize how insurers evaluate wind-resistant features. Every Florida-licensed homeowners insurer is legally required to accept this form and apply discounts based on its findings.

The form covers six feature categories: roof covering, roof deck attachment, roof-to-wall connection, roof shape, opening protection, and secondary water resistance. Each category has a tiered rating. The higher the tier, the larger the credit. A roof replacement directly affects at least three of these categories and sometimes all four of the roof-specific ones, which is why replacing an old roof can move your premium more than any other single home improvement.

The current version of the form (revised 2012, still in use as of 2026) is the same document your inspector will complete and sign. You can download a blank copy from the Florida OIR website to understand what the inspector will evaluate before they arrive.

## Roof Features That Qualify for the Largest Credits

Not every new roof earns the same discount. The credits depend on specific construction details — some of which your contractor can choose, and some of which are mandated by code.

**Roof covering (Section A):** A new roof using FBC-approved shingles, tile, or metal automatically qualifies for the base credit. To reach the maximum credit in this category, the installation must meet the requirements of the current Florida Building Code (FBC 7th Edition, 2020, for most jurisdictions) and have proper product approval documentation.

**Roof deck attachment (Section B):** This is often the single highest-value category. Older homes built before 2002 commonly have 6-inch nail spacing on the roof deck; current FBC requires 6d or 8d nails at 6 inches in the field and 6 inches at the edges for most wind zones. If your contractor upgrades to 8d ring-shank nails at 6/6 spacing — which costs roughly $300-$500 more in nails and labor on an average roof — you can move from a "B" rating (6-inch nail spacing) to an "A" rating (8d ring-shank nails, 6/6 pattern), which can add several hundred dollars per year in premium savings on its own.

**Roof-to-wall connection (Section C):** This category rewards hurricane straps and clips that tie the roof framing to the wall framing. In most Florida new construction since 2002, single wraps or clips are already installed. If you're in an HVHZ county (Miami-Dade or Broward), double wraps are required. This section is typically not changed during a standard reroof unless structural work is also done, but your inspector will still document what's there.

**Secondary water resistance (Section E):** If your reroofing project included peel-and-stick self-adhering modified bitumen underlayment — commonly called "peel-and-stick" or SWR — across the entire roof deck, this qualifies for the SWR credit. Standard felt underlayment does not qualify. The upgrade typically costs $400-$900 more on a standard single-family home and can knock 5-10% off your wind premium on its own. In HVHZ counties, SWR is required by code, so there's no additional cost for that credit.

**Roof shape (Section D):** A hip roof — one that slopes on all four sides — earns the maximum credit in this category. A gable roof earns nothing. You won't change your roof shape during a standard replacement, but if you're doing a substantial addition or complete rebuild, it's worth knowing that hip roofs can produce premium savings of $500-$1,500 per year compared to gable roofs in high-wind zones.

## What Credits Actually Look Like in Dollar Terms

Florida law requires insurers to give credits, but the amount varies by insurer, location, and coverage amount. That said, real-world results from completed OIR-B1-1802 inspections in Florida give a reasonable picture:

- A 2,000 sq ft home in Tampa (Hillsborough County) with a new hip roof, 8d ring-shank nails at 6/6, SWR underlayment, and impact-rated windows can see annual premium reductions of $1,800-$3,200 depending on the insurer and the home's replacement cost value.
- A similar home on the Space Coast (Brevard County) with the same features has seen reductions of $1,200-$2,400.
- In Miami-Dade HVHZ, where virtually all construction features are already code-mandated, the credits are still meaningful but the delta between a pre-code and post-code roof is smaller — typically $800-$1,500 per year — because the baseline rating was already higher.

The inspection fee ($75-$150) pays for itself in the first month of premium savings in almost every case.

## The Inspection Timeline: How Long Between Roof and Credit

This is where homeowners lose money through inaction. The sequence matters.

**Final permit inspection first.** No county inspector has signed off yet? The wind mitigation inspector can't legally certify the work. Don't schedule your OIR-B1-1802 inspection until the permit has a final approval. Most counties process final roofing inspections within 3-10 business days of a contractor's request.

**Wind mitigation inspection within 30 days of permit closure.** There's no legal requirement to rush, but every month you delay is a month you're paying un-credited premium. Schedule the wind mitigation inspection the same week the final permit passes.

**OIR-B1-1802 submittal to your insurer immediately after.** Insurers must apply credits retroactively to your current policy period under Florida Statute 627.0629. If you replaced your roof in January and don't submit the form until October, you're entitled to a credit back to January — but you have to ask for it and confirm the carrier processed it correctly.

The OIR-B1-1802 report is valid for five years, after which your insurer can require a re-inspection (though the form doesn't expire mid-policy for most carriers).

## How to Get Your Florida Wind Mitigation Credit After a Roof Replacement

Follow these six steps in order. Skipping or reordering them is the most common reason homeowners see delayed or denied credits.

1. **Replace or repair your roof to current Florida Building Code standards.** Any roof replacement must meet FBC Chapter 15 requirements for your county. In High-Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ) counties — Miami-Dade and Broward — additional Product Approval requirements apply. Make sure your contractor pulls the required permit; no permit means no valid mitigation inspection.

2. **Gather documentation from your roofing contractor.** Collect the permit number, a copy of the Notice of Commencement, the product approval numbers for the roofing system, and any manufacturer specs for the roof deck attachment (nail type, spacing, and pattern). Your contractor should provide all of this at project closeout.

3. **Wait for the final permit inspection to pass.** The county or municipality must issue a final approval on the roofing permit before a wind mitigation inspector can verify the work. This typically takes 3-10 business days after the contractor calls for final inspection.

4. **Hire a licensed wind mitigation inspector.** In Florida, wind mitigation inspections must be performed by a licensed home inspector, general contractor, building contractor, roofing contractor, or engineer. Fees run $75-$150 for a standard single-family home. The inspector photographs and documents every qualifying feature on the OIR-B1-1802 form.

5. **Submit the completed OIR-B1-1802 form to your insurance carrier.** Send the signed and stamped OIR-B1-1802 report to your homeowners insurance agent. Insurers are required by Florida law to apply the credits retroactively to your current policy period. Ask your agent to run a re-rate immediately — don't wait for your renewal date.

6. **Review your updated declarations page and verify the credits applied.** Within 30 days, request an updated declarations page. Confirm each credit line item matches what the OIR-B1-1802 documented. If a credit is missing or lower than expected, ask your agent for the underwriter's specific reason in writing.

## What Happens If Your Contractor Didn't Pull a Permit

No permit, no inspection, no credit. More importantly: no permit means the work may not be insurable at all. Florida homeowners insurance carriers increasingly audit claims by cross-referencing county permit records. An unpermitted roof replacement can result in a claim denial after a hurricane — even if the roof itself performs fine — because the insurer can argue the work was not performed to code.

If you discover after the fact that your roofer didn't pull a permit, contact your local building department immediately. Some counties allow homeowners to pull an "after-the-fact" permit, which triggers a code inspection. If the installation passes, you're back on track. If it fails, the county can require a partial or full tear-off and redo. This scenario plays out in Florida every year, particularly after unlicensed storm-chaser contractors move through a region following a major hurricane.

## Common Mistakes That Reduce or Eliminate Wind Mitigation Credits

**Using standard felt underlayment instead of SWR.** It's a cheaper option, but the insurer won't give you the secondary water resistance credit. The difference in material cost is $400-$900. The difference in lifetime premium savings is often $3,000-$8,000 over a 10-year policy cycle.

**Not specifying nail type and spacing in the contract.** If your contractor doesn't document that 8d ring-shank nails were used at 6/6 spacing, the inspector has to assume the lower-rated option. Make sure your contract specifies nail type, length, and pattern before work starts — not after.

**Submitting the form to the wrong department.** Send the OIR-B1-1802 to your agent, not the insurer's claims department. Claims processors don't handle underwriting credits. If your agent seems unfamiliar with the process, escalate to the insurer's underwriting department directly.

**Letting the report expire without renewal.** The five-year validity period catches homeowners off guard. Put a calendar reminder for four years and eleven months from your inspection date so you can schedule a re-inspection before any policy renewal that might require it.

# San Antonio Roof Replacement Permit Process Explained

Pulling a permit for a roof replacement in San Antonio is not optional — and skipping it creates real problems: voided manufacturer warranties, insurance claim denials, and code violations that surface at the worst possible moment, like during a home sale. Here's exactly how the process works, what it costs, and what your contractor is responsible for handling.

## Why San Antonio Requires a Permit for Roof Replacement

The City of San Antonio adopted the 2021 International Residential Code (IRC), which classifies a full roof replacement as a structural alteration requiring a permit and inspection. The permit process exists so a licensed inspector can verify that the decking, underlayment, flashing, and shingle installation meet minimum wind and weather resistance standards — important in a region that sees hail seasons from March through June and periodic tropical moisture from Gulf systems.

A reroof done without a permit may still look fine from the street. The problems show up when:

- Your homeowners insurance denies a future storm claim because the work was unpermitted
- A home buyer's inspector flags the open or missing permit during closing
- GAF, Owens Corning, or another manufacturer refuses a warranty claim tied to an uninspected installation

Permits also protect you if a contractor cuts corners on decking repairs or underlayment — the inspector catches it before the shingles go on.

## City of San Antonio vs. Bexar County — Which Applies to You

This is the first thing to sort out, because the two jurisdictions have separate portals, offices, and fee schedules.

**City of San Antonio (inside city limits):** Permits go through the Development Services Department (DSD). Online submission is available at sapermits.sanantonio.gov. Walk-in service is at 1901 S. Alamo St., open Monday–Friday 7:45 AM–4:30 PM.

**Unincorporated Bexar County (outside city limits):** Permits go through Bexar County Development Services at 3003 Judson Road, Suite 100. This applies to communities like Helotes, Converse, Schertz, and parts of Live Oak that sit outside San Antonio city limits.

Check your property's jurisdiction at bcad.org by searching your address and reviewing the appraisal district record. If you're unsure, your contractor should know — it's a standard pre-job step.

## How to Pull a Roof Replacement Permit in San Antonio

### Step 1: Confirm jurisdiction — city or county

Determine whether your property falls under City of San Antonio jurisdiction or unincorporated Bexar County. Properties inside city limits use the City of San Antonio Development Services Department (DSD). Properties outside city limits but within Bexar County use Bexar County Development Services. Your county appraisal district record at bcad.org will show which applies.

### Step 2: Gather required documents

Prepare a completed permit application, a site plan or property sketch showing the structure, and a scope-of-work description specifying the roofing material, deck repair extent, and square footage. For reroofs over existing shingles, note the number of existing layers being removed.

### Step 3: Submit the permit application

City of San Antonio permits can be submitted online through the AMANDA portal at sapermits.sanantonio.gov or in person at 1901 S. Alamo St. Bexar County permits are submitted at 3003 Judson Road, Suite 100. Licensed contractors can submit on your behalf — and typically must if they are pulling the permit under their license.

### Step 4: Pay the permit fee

City of San Antonio residential reroof permit fees are calculated based on project valuation. For a typical 2,000–2,500 sq ft home, expect $100–$225. Bexar County fees follow a similar valuation schedule and generally fall in the $125–$350 range depending on project size. Fees are paid at time of submission.

### Step 5: Schedule the post-installation inspection

Once the roof is complete, the permit holder (your contractor) requests an inspection through the city or county portal. The City of San Antonio DSD typically schedules inspections within 1–3 business days. The inspector checks decking, underlayment, flashing, ridge cap, and fastener patterns against the 2021 International Residential Code (IRC) as adopted by Texas.

### Step 6: Receive inspection approval and close the permit

If the inspection passes, the permit is marked closed in the system. Keep a copy of the closed permit and inspection record — you'll need it for homeowners insurance documentation and when you sell the property. If corrections are required, your contractor must address them and request a re-inspection before the permit closes.

## What Your Contractor Handles vs. What You Need to Know

A licensed Texas roofing contractor pulls the permit under their TDLR (Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation) license number. That means the permit is issued in their name, and they bear responsibility for the work passing inspection. You should not be asked to pull your own permit for contractor-performed work — that's a red flag.

Your role as the homeowner:

- Verify the contractor is TDLR-licensed before signing anything (search at tdlr.texas.gov)
- Confirm in writing that the permit fee is included in the contract price (most reputable contractors bundle it in)
- Be home or available when the inspector arrives, or confirm the contractor will be on-site
- Request a copy of the closed permit once the job is complete

## Timeline: How Long Does the Permit Process Add?

For most San Antonio residential reroofs, the permit process adds 1–3 business days to the project timeline — not weeks. Here's the typical sequence:

- **Day 1:** Contractor submits permit application online (same-day approval is common for standard reroofs with no structural changes)
- **Days 2–5:** Tear-off, decking repairs, underlayment, and shingle installation
- **Days 6–8:** Contractor requests inspection; inspection typically occurs within 1–3 business days
- **Day 9–10:** Permit closed upon passing inspection

If your roof has structural complexity — a steep pitch over 6:12, multiple penetrations, or significant decking replacement — factor in an extra day for inspection scheduling during busy spring storm season.

## What the Inspector Actually Checks

San Antonio DSD inspectors verify compliance with the 2021 IRC and applicable local amendments. On a standard asphalt shingle reroof, the inspection covers:

- **Decking condition:** No soft spots, delamination, or unrepaired rot before shingles go on
- **Underlayment:** Proper overlap and fastening (IRC R905.1 governs underlayment for asphalt shingles)
- **Drip edge:** Required at both eaves and rakes under IRC R905.2.8.5
- **Flashing:** Step flashing at wall intersections, counter-flashing at chimneys, proper valley treatment
- **Shingle fastening:** Minimum four nails per shingle in the nailing zone, or six nails in high-wind conditions
- **Ridge cap:** Proper overlap and fastening pattern

Inspectors do not check the quality of the shingles themselves or verify the brand — that's between you and your contractor's warranty.

## Common Reasons Reroofs Fail Inspection

Most failed inspections in San Antonio come down to three issues:

1. **Missing or improperly installed drip edge** — contractors skipping drip edge to save time or cost
2. **Inadequate flashing at penetrations** — pipe boots, skylights, or HVAC curbs not properly sealed
3. **Improper nail placement** — nails driven too high (in the exposure zone) or not enough fasteners per shingle

Each of these requires the contractor to correct the deficiency and schedule a re-inspection, which delays final permit closure. When reviewing contractor bids, ask specifically whether drip edge replacement and proper flashing are included — vague scopes of work are where corners get cut.

# Filing a Storm Damage Roof Claim in Louisville, KY

Louisville sits in the Ohio Valley corridor where Gulf moisture collides with cold fronts rolling off the Appalachians — a recipe for some of the most frequent large-hail events in the eastern United States. Jefferson County averages 4–6 significant hail events per year, and the spring window of March through June accounts for roughly 70% of them. When one of those storms crosses your neighborhood, the clock on your insurance claim starts immediately, even if the damage isn't obvious from the street.

## Why Louisville Roofs Take a Harder Hit Than Most

Hailstones in the Ohio Valley tend to be dense and fast-moving. The Louisville NWS office has recorded hail events exceeding 2 inches in diameter in Jefferson County multiple times in the past decade — large enough to fracture asphalt shingles, crack ridge caps, and dent aluminum flashing on a single pass. Wind events compound the problem: Louisville's position between the Appalachians and the open plains means straight-line winds regularly reach 60–70 mph even in non-tornado events.

The 2021 April storm cluster that moved through the Highlands, Crescent Hill, and St. Matthews neighborhoods is a useful reference point. NOAA recorded 1.75-inch hail and 64 mph gusts. Insurers in Jefferson County paid out thousands of residential claims from that single three-day event. If your home was built before 2010, it almost certainly has asphalt shingles that predate current impact-resistance standards — meaning the damage threshold is lower than on newer construction.

## What Kentucky Law Requires of Insurers

Kentucky's Department of Insurance regulates claim timelines under KRS Chapter 304. Key deadlines your insurer must follow:

- **10 days** to acknowledge receipt of your claim
- **15 days** to accept or deny coverage after receiving your proof of loss (or request additional information)
- **15 additional days** to respond once any requested information is received

If your insurer misses these windows without written explanation, file a complaint with the Kentucky DOI at insurance.ky.gov. Kentucky does not cap the claim amount at your ACV if you have RCV coverage and complete repairs within a reasonable timeframe — typically defined as 180 days from the date of loss, though many policies allow extensions on written request.

## The KY 5-Day Rescission Right — What It Actually Means

Under **KRS 304.20-320**, when you sign a roofing contract that is contingent on insurance proceeds, you have **5 business days** to cancel that contract without any financial penalty, even after your claim is approved and even if work has been scheduled.

This law exists because storm chasers — out-of-state contractors who follow major weather events — often pressure homeowners into signing contracts before they fully understand their coverage. The rescission right gives you a cooling-off period. To invoke it, written notice sent by certified mail within 5 business days is sufficient. The contractor cannot charge a cancellation fee, cannot retain a deposit, and cannot file a lien for work not yet performed.

The law does **not** protect you if you signed a standard (non-contingent) roofing contract. Know what you're signing.

## How to File Your Claim: Step-by-Step

### Step 1: Document the damage within 24–48 hours of the storm

Walk the perimeter of your home and photograph every visible sign of damage from ground level — dented gutters, missing shingles, granule accumulation in downspout splash blocks. Check your attic for daylight or new water stains. Do not climb onto the roof yourself. Timestamp all photos and note the storm date.

### Step 2: Pull the NOAA storm report for your ZIP code

Go to NOAA's Storm Events Database (ncdc.noaa.gov/stormevents) and search Jefferson County, KY for the storm date. Download or screenshot the event record — it lists hail size, wind speed, and affected area. Insurers treat NOAA records as independent verification, and having it ready speeds up adjuster review.

### Step 3: Review your policy before you call your insurer

Locate your declarations page and identify three things: your dwelling deductible (many Kentucky policies now carry a separate 1%–2% wind/hail deductible), whether coverage is ACV (Actual Cash Value) or RCV (Replacement Cost Value), and the notice-of-loss deadline. Most Kentucky homeowner policies require notice within a 'reasonable time,' but filing within 7 days of the storm is best practice.

### Step 4: File your claim and request a licensed public adjuster or contractor inspection first

Call your insurer's claims line to open the claim and get a claim number. Before the insurance adjuster arrives, schedule a licensed roofing contractor to inspect and write a scope of loss. Kentucky does not prohibit assignment-of-benefits arrangements, but you are not required to use one — an independent written scope simply gives you a second data point when the adjuster's estimate arrives.

### Step 5: Meet the insurance adjuster on-site with your documentation

Be present when the adjuster inspects. Bring your contractor's scope of loss, the NOAA storm report, and your timestamped photos. Point out every area documented. If the adjuster's estimate is lower than the contractor's scope, note specific line-item discrepancies in writing before signing anything.

### Step 6: Invoke the Kentucky 5-day rescission right if you sign a contractor agreement

Under KRS 304.20-320, if you sign a roofing contract contingent on insurance proceeds, you have 5 business days to rescind that contract without penalty — even after the claim is approved. Read the contract carefully before signing. If you change your mind for any reason within that window, send written notice by certified mail.

### Step 7: Negotiate or invoke appraisal if estimates diverge significantly

If your insurer's estimate and the contractor's scope differ by more than 10%, request an itemized explanation. Most Kentucky homeowner policies include an appraisal clause: each party hires an independent appraiser, the two appraisers choose a neutral umpire, and the umpire's decision is binding. This process typically takes 30–60 days and costs $300–$800 per side.

### Step 8: Schedule repairs and confirm your RCV holdback

Once the claim is approved, the insurer releases the ACV payment (replacement cost minus depreciation). After repairs are complete, submit the final invoice and request the depreciation holdback — called the 'recoverable depreciation' release. Most Kentucky insurers release this within 10–15 business days of receiving proof of completion.

## ACV vs. RCV: The Difference Is Thousands of Dollars

On a typical Louisville ranch home with a 1,800 sq ft roof, the gap between ACV and RCV settlement can be $3,500–$6,000. ACV policies subtract depreciation — an older roof might be depreciated 40–60%, leaving the homeowner responsible for a significant portion out of pocket beyond the deductible. RCV policies pay the full replacement cost once repairs are completed.

If you're on an ACV policy and your roof is more than 15 years old, it's worth calling your insurer before storm season to ask about upgrading to RCV coverage. The premium difference is often $150–$300 per year — less than one year's depreciation on a roof claim.

## Common Mistakes That Delay or Reduce Kentucky Claims

**Waiting too long to document.** Granule loss and shingle bruising from hail can look identical to normal weathering 30 days later. An adjuster who arrives late can legitimately argue pre-existing wear.

**Signing with a storm chaser before reading the contract.** Out-of-state contractors have no Kentucky contractor license obligation and often disappear after collecting the ACV check. Verify any contractor's Kentucky license at labor.ky.gov before signing.

**Accepting the first estimate without a second scope.** Insurance adjusters are not adversaries, but they do work from software (typically Xactimate) that can undervalue certain line items — flashing replacement, drip edge, and code-upgrade costs are frequently missed on initial estimates.

**Missing the recoverable depreciation deadline.** Most policies require you to submit for depreciation release within 180 days of the claim approval. If repairs are delayed, call your insurer and request a written extension before the window closes.

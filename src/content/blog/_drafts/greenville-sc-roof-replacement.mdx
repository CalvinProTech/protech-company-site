# Roof Replacement in Greenville, SC: Costs, Materials, and Timeline

Greenville homeowners replacing a roof in 2026 are looking at $7,500–$18,000 for most single-family homes — but where you land in that range depends on square footage, pitch, material choice, and whether you're dealing with any underlying deck damage. This is a breakdown of what drives those numbers in Upstate South Carolina specifically, why Greenville's climate calls for different decisions than the Charleston coast, and what a typical project looks like from first call to final cleanup.

## What Roof Replacement Costs in Greenville

For a 1,500–2,000 sq ft ranch or two-story home in Greenville, the all-in cost for a standard architectural shingle roof runs $9,000–$13,500. Larger homes in the 2,500–3,000 sq ft range — common in neighborhoods like Botany Woods, Chanticleer, and Augusta Road — typically land between $13,000 and $18,000.

The per-square cost (roofing is priced in "squares," each equal to 100 sq ft of roof surface) breaks down roughly like this:

- **Architectural asphalt shingles**: $380–$520 per square installed
- **Impact-resistant (Class 4) shingles**: $450–$600 per square installed
- **Standing seam metal**: $800–$1,100 per square installed
- **Synthetic slate or shake**: $700–$950 per square installed

Those ranges include tear-off of one layer, new underlayment, drip edge, ridge cap, and basic flashing. Add $500–$1,200 if there's a second layer of old shingles to remove. Deck repair — replacing rotted OSB sheathing — runs $65–$90 per sheet, and it's common to find 5–15 damaged sheets on a roof that's been leaking for a season or two.

Permit fees in Greenville County are typically $150–$300 for a residential re-roof. South Carolina's building code (2018 IBC with state amendments) requires permits for full replacements — any contractor who suggests skipping one is cutting a corner that could affect your homeowner's insurance claim down the road.

## Greenville vs. Coastal SC: Why the Spec Is Different

Coastal South Carolina — Charleston, Myrtle Beach, Hilton Head — sits within the South Carolina High Velocity Hurricane Zone, which mandates specific wind uplift requirements, self-sealing shingles rated to 130+ mph, and enhanced fastening patterns. Greenville is not in that zone.

That said, Upstate SC has its own weather profile that shapes material choices:

- **Ice and snow**: Greenville averages 4–6 inches of snowfall annually, and ice storms hit the Upstate harder than anywhere on the coast. Ridge ice damming is a real failure point — roofs need proper ventilation and a quality ice-and-water shield in the first 3 feet from the eave, per IRC R905.1.2.
- **Wind events**: Greenville sees thunderstorm wind gusts of 60–80 mph several times a year. The April 2020 derecho caused widespread damage across the Upstate — Class 3 or Class 4 impact-resistant shingles pay for themselves here, and many insurers offer a premium discount for them.
- **Heat and UV load**: Summers in Greenville are hot and humid. Shingles rated for algae resistance (look for the AR designation or shingles containing copper granules) are worth specifying — the dark staining you see on older Greenville roofs is almost always algae, not dirt, and it degrades the shingle surface over time.

A coastal spec for Greenville wastes money on wind-rated products you don't need. A Greenville spec for the coast would fail the permit inspection. They're genuinely different jobs.

## Which Materials Make Sense for Upstate SC

**Architectural asphalt shingles** are the right call for most Greenville homeowners. A GAF Timberline HDZ or Owens Corning Duration shingle, properly installed with a manufacturer-approved system, gives you a 30-year functional lifespan in Greenville's climate, strong wind resistance (Class H, 130 mph), and a transferable warranty that adds value at resale. These are the workhorse of the Upstate market and for good reason.

**Class 4 impact-resistant shingles** — products like GAF Timberline AS II or Malarkey Vista AR — cost $70–$150 more per square but can earn a 10–30% discount on your homeowner's insurance premium with some carriers. Run the math: on a 20-square roof, that's $1,400–$3,000 in upfront cost, offset by $200–$600/year in savings. The break-even is 5–8 years, and these shingles typically carry a 50-year warranty.

**Standing seam metal** is the premium option for Greenville homeowners who plan to stay long-term. It handles Upstate ice events better than any shingle product, has a 40–70 year lifespan, and sheds snow cleanly. The cost is roughly double architectural shingles, but you're also likely never replacing it again. It's particularly common on steeper-pitched craftsman and farmhouse-style homes in areas like Paris Mountain and the North Main corridor.

**Synthetic slate** splits the difference — the look of slate without the 10–12 lb/sq ft dead load that most Greenville homes weren't framed to carry. Products like DaVinci Roofscapes or Brava carry 50-year warranties and perform well in Upstate freeze-thaw cycles.

## How Long Roof Replacement Takes in Greenville

From signed contract to finished job, budget 2–4 weeks for most projects. That timeline breaks down as follows:

- **Permit pull**: Greenville County typically processes residential roofing permits in 3–7 business days.
- **Material delivery**: Most shingle orders arrive within 2–5 business days of permit approval. Metal roofing panels, if custom-cut, can take 2–3 weeks.
- **Installation**: A standard 20–25 square asphalt shingle roof takes 1–2 days with a full crew. Metal roofing takes 3–5 days. Unexpected deck repairs add a day.
- **Inspection**: Greenville County requires a final inspection after re-roofing. Scheduling typically takes 2–4 business days after the contractor calls it in.

Weather delays are real in the Upstate — summer afternoon thunderstorms can push a schedule by a day or two. Most reputable contractors build a weather buffer into their contracts. A project that starts in early July should plan for completion no later than the third week.

## What the Tear-Off and Installation Process Looks Like

On day one, the crew sets up tarps over landscaping and HVAC equipment, then removes the existing shingles and disposes of them — a 20-square tear-off generates 2–3 tons of debris, typically hauled in a dedicated dumpster or trailer. The deck gets inspected for soft spots, rot, or delamination before anything new goes down.

New underlayment goes on first: a synthetic underlayment (30-lb felt equivalent or better) over the field of the roof, with ice-and-water shield at eaves and valleys. Drip edge is installed along the eave before underlayment and along rakes after — this sequencing matters for water management and is specifically addressed in IBC Section 1507.

Shingles are installed starting at the eave and working up, with a 4-nail minimum fastening pattern per South Carolina code. Ridge vents are cut and installed before the ridge cap goes on. Flashing at chimneys, skylights, and walls is either re-used (if still sound) or replaced — flashing replacement typically adds $200–$800 depending on complexity.

## How to Evaluate Bids from Greenville Roofers

Get three written estimates. They should each itemize: tear-off cost, disposal, underlayment type, shingle brand and product name, flashing scope, permit, and warranty terms. If a bid just says "roof replacement — $10,500" with no line items, ask for the breakdown before signing.

Verify the contractor's South Carolina residential contractor license (search the SC LLR database at llr.sc.gov) and confirm they carry general liability and workers' comp. An uninsured crew on your roof is a homeowner's insurance problem waiting to happen.

GAF Master Elite certification — held by roughly 2% of roofing contractors nationally — requires ongoing training and licensing verification, and qualifies the homeowner for GAF's enhanced System Plus and Golden Pledge warranties. Standard contractor warranties max out at the manufacturer's base coverage; Master Elite contractors can offer 25–50 year enhanced warranties that are transferable to future buyers.

## What the Final Inspection Covers

Greenville County's final inspection checks that the installation matches the permitted scope, that flashings are properly integrated, that ventilation meets code, and that drip edge is correctly installed. Inspectors may also check that required permits are posted and that the correct shingle class was installed. Your contractor should be present or available by phone during the inspection.

A failed inspection is uncommon when the crew is experienced, but it does happen — typically for improper fastening patterns or missing flashing at a penetration. The fix is usually same-day, and the re-inspection is rescheduled within a few days.

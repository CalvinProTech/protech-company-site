# Roof Financing in 2026: Loans, HELOCs, and Manufacturer Programs Compared

A new roof on a 2,000 sq ft home runs $9,000–$18,000 depending on materials, slope, and region. Most homeowners don't have that sitting in a checking account, and they shouldn't have to. The financing options available in 2026 are better than they've been in years — but the differences between them matter more than most contractors will tell you. Here's a straight comparison so you can pick the right one before the estimate even lands in your inbox.

## The Four Main Ways to Finance a Roof

Most homeowners end up choosing between four options:

1. **Contractor-arranged financing** (GreenSky, Synchrony Home, or similar point-of-sale lenders)
2. **Home equity line of credit (HELOC)**
3. **Personal loan** from a bank, credit union, or online lender
4. **Cash / savings**

Each has a different cost structure, approval timeline, and risk profile. None is universally best.

## GreenSky: Fast Approval, Promo Periods, Watch the Back-End Rate

GreenSky is one of the most common financing tools offered through roofing contractors. It runs a soft credit pull for pre-qualification and a hard pull at funding. Approval takes 2–5 minutes at the job site or online.

**Current rate range (2026):** 6.99%–29.99% APR depending on creditworthiness and loan term.

**Promo offers:** GreenSky frequently runs 12-month and 18-month deferred-interest promotions — meaning 0% if the balance is paid in full before the promo period ends. If it isn't, the deferred interest from the entire purchase period posts to your account at once. On a $12,000 roof with an 18-month promo at 26.99% back-end rate, that can add $2,500–$3,000 overnight if you carry even a small balance.

**Qualification:** Generally requires a 600+ FICO score for standard plans; 680+ for the best promo rates. Income verification is minimal on smaller loans under $15,000.

**Loan terms:** 24–120 months. Monthly payment on $12,000 at 9.99% over 60 months is roughly $255.

**Best for:** Homeowners with 680+ credit who can realistically pay off the balance during a promo period, or who want a fixed monthly payment with a mid-range rate and minimal paperwork.

## Synchrony Home: Retail Credit Card Model

Synchrony Home operates more like a home-improvement credit card than a traditional installment loan. Contractors who accept Synchrony issue you a credit card with a home-improvement-only spending limit.

**Current rate range (2026):** 26.99% standard APR, with 12–24 month deferred-interest promos common for qualified buyers.

**Approval:** Instant decision, soft pull for pre-qual, hard pull at application. Available with a 620+ FICO in most cases.

**Key difference from GreenSky:** The revolving credit structure means you can carry a balance after the project — but at 26.99%, that gets expensive fast. A $10,000 balance at 26.99% costs about $225/month in interest alone.

**Best for:** Homeowners who need approval quickly and can pay off the balance within the promo window. Not a good fit if there's any chance you'll carry a balance beyond the promotional period.

## HELOCs: Lowest Rates, But You're Borrowing Against Your Home

A home equity line of credit lets you borrow against the equity you've built up. If your home is worth $350,000 and you owe $200,000, you likely have enough equity to qualify.

**Current rate range (2026):** 7.25%–10.50% APR for qualified borrowers (variable, tied to prime rate). That's consistently lower than any contractor-arranged financing.

**Qualification:** Typically requires 80% combined loan-to-value ratio (meaning you can borrow up to 80% of your home's appraised value minus what you owe), 680+ FICO, and documented income. Some lenders go to 90% LTV at higher rates.

**Timeline:** 3–6 weeks from application to funding. If your roof is actively leaking, a HELOC isn't going to save you — but it's the right tool for a planned replacement.

**Tax deductibility:** Interest on a HELOC used for home improvement is deductible if you itemize, under IRS Publication 936. Verify with your tax advisor — this is not universal.

**Risk:** Your home is collateral. Default has consequences that a GreenSky default doesn't.

**Best for:** Homeowners with solid equity, a 680+ FICO, and 4+ weeks before they need the work done. The interest savings over a 5-year period compared to a 9.99% contractor loan on $12,000 are roughly $1,800–$2,400.

## Personal Loans: No Collateral, Higher Rates

An unsecured personal loan through a bank, credit union (try your own first — member rates are usually better), or online lender like LightStream or SoFi doesn't require home equity.

**Current rate range (2026):** 8.99%–24.99% APR, depending heavily on credit score and income. LightStream's home improvement loans start around 8.99% for excellent credit (750+).

**Approval timeline:** 1–3 business days. Funds typically arrive in 1–5 business days after approval.

**Loan amounts:** $5,000–$100,000, though most lenders cap home improvement personal loans at $40,000–$50,000.

**No deferred-interest traps:** Unlike GreenSky or Synchrony promo plans, a personal loan has a fixed rate and fixed payment from day one. You always know what you owe.

**Best for:** Homeowners without significant equity, those who don't want to put their home up as collateral, and borrowers with 720+ credit who can qualify for the lower end of rates. Credit unions often beat banks by 1–2 percentage points — worth a call before going online.

## Paying Cash: Still the Best Deal, With One Caveat

If you have $12,000–$18,000 in savings earning 4.5%–5.0% in a high-yield account, the math gets interesting. A GreenSky loan at 9.99% costs you about 5 percentage points more than what your savings earn. On $12,000 over 5 years, that's roughly $3,000 in net interest cost.

That said, cash is only the right answer if it doesn't drain your emergency fund below 3 months of expenses. Depleting your savings for a roof and then financing a furnace replacement at 19.99% three months later is not a win.

**Contractor discounts for cash:** Some contractors offer 2%–5% off for cash payment, which improves the math further. It's always worth asking.

## What to Watch Out For in Any Financing Agreement

**Deferred interest vs. true 0% interest.** These are not the same. True 0% means no interest accrues. Deferred interest means interest accrues but is waived if you pay off the balance in full. Most contractor-arranged promos are deferred interest — read the disclosure.

**Prepayment penalties.** Rare in 2026, but check. Some personal loans and a few contractor financing products charge 1%–2% of the remaining balance if you pay early.

**Origination fees.** Personal loans sometimes charge 1%–6% of the loan amount upfront. A $12,000 loan with a 3% origination fee costs you $360 before the first payment. Factor this into your APR comparison.

**Who's actually lending.** GreenSky originates through regional banks (Goldman Sachs has been involved historically); Synchrony is its own bank. If your contractor goes out of business, your loan obligation doesn't disappear — you still owe the lender.

## Which Option Makes Sense in Your Situation

- **Roof is leaking and you need it done this week:** GreenSky or Synchrony for speed. Take the promo if you can realistically pay it off. Take the fixed installment plan if you can't.
- **You have 4+ weeks and solid equity:** HELOC, no question. Lowest rate, potentially deductible.
- **You want predictable payments without touching your equity:** Personal loan from a credit union or LightStream.
- **You have the cash and a healthy emergency fund:** Pay cash and ask for a discount.
- **You have sub-620 credit:** Options narrow fast. Some GreenSky and Synchrony programs go to 580 with a co-applicant. FHA Title I loans (government-backed, for home improvements) are worth looking at — lenders can approve down to 580 FICO with a maximum $25,000 unsecured.

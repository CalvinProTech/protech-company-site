# DFW Hail Damage Roof Repair: A North Texas Homeowner's Guide

North Texas sits dead center in what meteorologists call "Hail Alley" — the corridor stretching from west Texas through the DFW Metroplex and into Oklahoma where supercell thunderstorms produce the most damaging hailstorms in the country. Between 2008 and 2023, the Dallas-Fort Worth area logged more than $15 billion in insured hail losses, according to the Insurance Council of Texas. If a storm has rolled through your zip code, there's a good chance your roof took a hit — even if you can't see it from the driveway.

This guide covers exactly how to spot hail damage on a DFW roof, how the Texas insurance claim process works from first call to final payment, and the one legal rule every North Texas homeowner must understand before signing anything with a roofing contractor.

## Why DFW Gets Hit So Hard

The Metroplex sits at the collision zone of three air masses: warm, moist Gulf air from the south; dry, hot air from the Chihuahuan Desert to the west; and cold Arctic outflows that push down through the plains from the north. When these collide in spring and early summer, they produce the rotating supercell thunderstorms that generate large hail — often baseball-sized or larger.

The worst DFW hailstorms on record include the April 2016 event that dropped 4.5-inch hailstones across Wylie, Garland, and Rowlett (causing $1.36 billion in insured losses in a single afternoon), and the March 2022 storm that hammered Burleson, Crowley, and south Fort Worth. These aren't once-in-a-generation events. The National Weather Service Fort Worth office recorded 47 significant hail events (1 inch diameter or larger) in Tarrant and Dallas counties in 2023 alone.

Peak hail season runs March through June, though severe cells can form as late as October. If your roof is more than 10 years old and hasn't been inspected after a major storm, there's a meaningful chance you're carrying unrepaired damage.

## How to Spot Hail Damage Before an Adjuster Arrives

Hail damage to asphalt shingles isn't always obvious. The most common indicators that get missed:

**Granule loss in circular patterns.** Hail knocks the embedded granules off shingles, leaving dark, exposed asphalt spots. These appear round or oval, not in the elongated lines you'd see from foot traffic. Check your gutters and downspout discharge areas — piles of granules after a storm are a reliable early indicator.

**Dented or dimpled soft metals.** Aluminum gutters, downspouts, drip edge (the metal strip along roof edges), pipe boots (the rubber or lead collars around plumbing vents), and AC condenser fins all dent easily from hail. Damage here is highly visible and makes for strong photographic evidence.

**Cracked or split shingles.** On older shingles, hail impact causes the mat beneath the granules to crack. Run your hand over suspect areas — a damaged shingle has a soft, bruised feel compared to adjacent undamaged shingles.

**Wood fence and trim damage.** Hail that's large enough to damage a roof will leave visible pockmarks on cedar fencing, windowsill trim, and wood fascia boards. These are easy to photograph from the ground.

**What you won't see easily:** functional damage to the shingle mat that reduces its weather resistance but doesn't look dramatic yet. That's why a professional inspection matters — an experienced roofer knows exactly where to look and what to probe.

## The Texas Insurance Claim Timeline

Texas Insurance Code §542A.003, which took effect September 1, 2019, sets a **1-year filing deadline** for residential first-party hail and wind claims. Miss that window and your insurer has grounds to deny the claim outright, regardless of how severe the damage is. Note the storm date and act within that year.

Once you file, the insurer has **15 days** to acknowledge the claim and begin investigation (Texas Insurance Code §542.055). They must accept or reject the claim within **15 business days** after receiving all requested documentation (§542.056), though they can extend this once with written notice.

After accepting the claim, payment must be issued within **5 business days** (§542.057). If the insurer misses any of these deadlines, Texas law requires them to pay 18% per year in interest plus attorney's fees — a meaningful penalty that insurers take seriously.

Most straightforward DFW hail claims resolve within 30-60 days from the first call to the initial ACV (actual cash value) check. Recoverable depreciation — the holdback the insurer releases after work is completed — typically arrives 2-4 weeks after you submit the certificate of completion.

## The Deductible Rule: What Every DFW Homeowner Must Know

This is the most important legal point in this guide, so read it carefully.

**In Texas, it is illegal for a roofing contractor to waive, absorb, rebate, or credit your insurance deductible.**

Texas Business & Commerce Code §707.002, effective September 1, 2019, makes it a Class B misdemeanor (first offense) and a Class A misdemeanor (subsequent offense) for a contractor to offer to cover a homeowner's deductible as an inducement to sign a contract. It's also insurance fraud under Texas Penal Code §35.02, which is a felony if the value exceeds $2,500.

You will encounter contractors — particularly storm-chasing crews that follow hail events into the Metroplex — who offer to "work with your deductible" or absorb it into the job cost. This scheme works by inflating the claim amount submitted to the insurer. When insurers detect it (and they increasingly do), they can deny the entire claim and cancel your policy. You, as the policyholder, bear legal exposure too.

If a contractor offers to waive your deductible: walk away. Report them to the Texas Department of Insurance at tdi.texas.gov.

A legitimate contractor will bill your insurer for the agreed scope of loss, collect the insurer's payment, and collect your deductible — separately, as required by your policy. That's the legal model.

## How to File a Hail Damage Roof Claim in DFW

Follow these steps in order. Skipping steps or doing them out of sequence is the most common reason claims get underpaid.

1. **Document the storm event.** Record the date, time, and zip code of the hailstorm. Pull the official storm report from NOAA's Storm Events Database or the National Weather Service Fort Worth office (weather.gov/fwd). Save screenshots showing hail size and storm track. Insurers use this data to verify your claim.

2. **Inspect from the ground and inside the attic.** Walk the perimeter and look for dented gutters, damaged AC condenser fins, cracked windowsill trim, and pockmarked wood fencing — all are reliable hail indicators visible without roof access. Inside, check your attic with a flashlight for pinhole light, new staining, or sagging decking.

3. **Photograph all visible damage.** Use your smartphone in good lighting. Capture gutters, downspouts, fascia, any skylights, and all soft-metal surfaces (drip edge, pipe boots, ridge cap). Do NOT get on the roof yourself — a licensed roofer documents roof-surface damage; your job is the perimeter and attic.

4. **Call your insurance company to open a claim.** Report the claim within 1 year of the storm date — Texas Insurance Code §542A.003 caps the filing window at 1 year for residential property claims filed after September 1, 2019. Have your storm documentation ready. The insurer will assign a claim number and schedule an adjuster.

5. **Get an independent roof inspection before the adjuster arrives.** Hire a licensed roofing contractor to inspect the roof and produce a written damage report before the insurance adjuster visits. This gives you an independent baseline. If the adjuster's scope of loss differs significantly from the contractor's report, you can invoke the appraisal clause in your policy.

6. **Review the adjuster's scope of loss.** Compare the adjuster's itemized estimate line by line against the contractor's report. Common omissions include: ridge cap replacement, pipe boot flashing, drip edge, and interior water damage. Dispute missing line items in writing, referencing your contractor's report and photos.

7. **Select a licensed Texas roofing contractor.** Verify the contractor holds a current Texas contractor registration if working in a municipality that requires it, carries general liability and workers' compensation insurance, and is authorized to install the shingle brand you're selecting. Get at least two written estimates.

8. **Sign a contract — never sign an Assignment of Benefits.** Texas HB 2102 (effective 2019) restricts Assignment of Benefits (AOB) contracts in property insurance. A legitimate contractor bills your insurer directly and collects your deductible from you — they do NOT waive, absorb, or credit your deductible. Any offer to "cover your deductible" is insurance fraud under Texas Penal Code §35.02 and can void your policy.

9. **Pay your deductible and schedule the repair.** Once the insurer issues payment (ACV check first, then recoverable depreciation after the work is complete), pay your deductible to the contractor and schedule installation. Most DFW roofing jobs take 1-2 days for a standard 2,000-3,000 sq ft home. Request a certificate of completion and all warranty documentation.

## What Hail Damage Repairs Actually Cost in DFW

For a full roof replacement on a typical 2,000-2,500 sq ft North Texas ranch or two-story home, expect material and labor to run **$9,500-$16,000** depending on pitch, shingle grade, and valley complexity. Class 4 impact-resistant shingles — products like GAF Timberline ArmorShield II or Owens Corning Duration Storm — run $1,500-$2,500 more than standard shingles but qualify for a 20-30% discount on homeowner's insurance premiums from most Texas carriers, which frequently pays back the premium within 3-5 years.

Partial repairs (replacing one or two slopes rather than the full roof) run $2,500-$6,000 but are only appropriate when the undamaged slopes have at least 8-10 years of remaining life. Mixing old and new shingles on adjacent slopes creates color mismatch and potential warranty complications — factors worth discussing with your contractor and adjuster before agreeing to a partial scope.

Your deductible is your out-of-pocket cost on an insurance claim. Texas policies increasingly carry wind/hail deductibles expressed as a percentage of insured value (1-3% is common) rather than a flat dollar amount. On a home insured for $350,000 with a 2% hail deductible, your out-of-pocket is $7,000 — before any insurance payment.

## Choosing a Contractor After a DFW Hailstorm

After a major storm, out-of-state storm chasers flood the Metroplex within 48-72 hours. They move fast, use high-pressure tactics, and often disappear before warranty issues surface. The warning signs:

- Asks you to sign a "direction to pay" or Assignment of Benefits before inspection
- Offers to handle your deductible
- Can't provide a Texas physical business address or local references
- Pressures you to sign on the day of the inspection
- Doesn't pull a permit (required in Dallas, Fort Worth, Arlington, Plano, and most other Metroplex municipalities for full roof replacements)

A legitimate contractor will pull the required permit, schedule the job with the city's inspection calendar, and give you a written contract that references the agreed scope of loss from your insurer's estimate. The permit protects you — it means a city inspector signs off on the installation, not just the contractor.

For work in Dallas County, Tarrant County, and surrounding areas, verify your contractor's license status through the relevant city permit office. Fort Worth requires a roofing contractor license; Dallas requires a permit pulled by a licensed general contractor or roofing contractor registered with the city.

# Kansas City Hail Damage Roof Repair: What Missouri Homeowners Should Know

Kansas City sits squarely in the Southern Plains hail corridor — a band stretching from Texas through Nebraska that produces more large-hail events per square mile than almost anywhere else in North America. In an average year, the KC metro experiences 6–8 storms that drop hail at or above 1 inch in diameter, the size insurance adjusters classify as potentially damaging to asphalt shingles. After a big storm rolls through Overland Park, Lee's Summit, or Liberty, the neighborhood fills with out-of-state storm chasers inside 72 hours. Knowing what real damage looks like, how Missouri's claim process actually works, and what your contract must contain legally will save you thousands.

## What Kansas City Hail Actually Does to a Roof

Not every hailstone wrecks a roof. Size, density, wind speed, and shingle age all factor in. Here's what damage looks like by hail size:

- **¾ inch (dime-sized):** Rarely damages modern architectural shingles but will crack brittle older 3-tab shingles and leave visible impressions on soft metals like aluminum gutters and flashing.
- **1 inch (quarter-sized):** The threshold where insurance adjusters begin writing structural damage. Expect granule displacement, visible dark bruising on shingles, and possible cracking of the fiberglass mat beneath.
- **1.5 inches (half-dollar):** High likelihood of full replacement on any shingle older than 10 years. Impact craters are visible from the ground on steep-slope roofs.
- **2 inches (golf ball) and above:** Structural sheathing damage, cracked skylights, and compromised ridge caps are common. Several storms per decade reach this threshold in the KC metro — including the May 2024 outbreak that tracked from Gardner through the Plaza.

The tricky part: granule loss and mat bruising are not always visible from the ground or even from a standing position on the roof. A roofer needs to press on suspect shingles and look for the soft, spongy feel that indicates the mat is fractured beneath an intact surface.

## How to Handle a Hail Damage Roof Claim in Kansas City

### Step 1: Document damage within 24–48 hours of the storm

Photograph every section of your roof from the ground using a zoom lens. Capture dented gutters, cracked skylight glazing, damaged AC condenser fins, and any visible bruising on shingles. Date-stamp everything. Do not walk the roof yourself — wet shingles after a storm are slippery, and a fall voids nothing but your health.

### Step 2: Request a written storm report

Pull a hail verification report from a service like NOAA's Storm Events Database or a paid source like CoreLogic WeatherVerify. Your insurance adjuster will have one; having your own copy lets you cross-reference dates and hail size. Kansas City sits in the Southern Plains hail corridor — storms producing 1-inch or larger hail (quarter-sized) hit the metro roughly 6–8 times per year.

### Step 3: Call your insurance carrier and open a claim

File the claim as soon as possible. Missouri has no statutory deadline for filing a first-party property claim, but most homeowner policies require "prompt notice." Waiting 60-plus days after a storm gives carriers grounds to dispute causation. Ask the carrier for the claim number, adjuster's name, and expected inspection timeline — typically 7–21 days in the KC metro after a widespread hail event.

### Step 4: Get a licensed roofing contractor to inspect before the adjuster arrives

A qualified roofer can identify functional damage that adjusters sometimes miss — granule loss in valleys, cracked mat beneath bruised shingles, compromised flashing seals. Have that contractor present a written scope of damage before you meet with the adjuster so you have a baseline to compare against the adjuster's estimate.

### Step 5: Review your contract for the Missouri 429.012 mechanic's-lien disclosure

Before signing any roofing contract in Missouri, confirm it contains the notice required by RSMo 429.012. This disclosure informs you that materialmen and subcontractors who are not paid by the general contractor can place a lien on your property even if you paid the contractor in full. Legitimate contractors include this language by law. If it's missing, walk away.

### Step 6: Negotiate the scope with your adjuster if needed

If the adjuster's estimate differs from your contractor's written scope, request a re-inspection or invoke your policy's appraisal clause. In Missouri, insurers must acknowledge a claim within 10 days and pay or deny within 15 days after proof of loss is submitted (RSMo 375.1008). If your carrier stalls beyond those windows, you have grounds to file a complaint with the Missouri Department of Insurance.

### Step 7: Schedule repairs and confirm material delivery before signing the final agreement

Once the claim is approved, confirm shingle model, color, and quantity with your contractor before work begins. For GAF products, verify the contractor is authorized to issue the warranty tier you're getting in writing. Repairs on most KC-area homes — 1,500–2,500 sq ft — take 1–2 days once materials arrive. Full replacement on a complex hip or mansard roof may run 3–4 days.

## Understanding Missouri's RSMo 429.012 — The Lien Disclosure You Must See

This statute is specific to Missouri and directly affects homeowners who pay for work with insurance proceeds. Here's what it requires in plain terms:

Any contractor performing residential improvements must provide a written disclosure notifying the homeowner that subcontractors and material suppliers who go unpaid by the general contractor have the legal right to file a mechanic's lien against your property — even if you've already paid the contractor in full.

Storm-chaser contractors — often out-of-state operations that follow major weather events — frequently skip this disclosure because they don't know Missouri law or because they're deliberately cutting corners. The omission matters: if a sub-contractor from an out-of-state crew places a lien on your Johnson County home six months after your "new roof" is finished, clearing that lien costs money and time and can complicate a refinance or sale.

What to look for on your contract: The disclosure should appear above the signature line in a conspicuous font, not buried in small print. If a contractor hands you a two-page estimate with no lien disclosure language and asks you to sign immediately, that's a reason to pause.

## What KC Homeowners Get Wrong About Actual Cash Value vs. Replacement Cost

Most standard homeowner policies in Missouri pay hail claims on one of two bases:

- **Replacement Cost Value (RCV):** The insurer pays what it costs to replace the damaged roof with like-kind materials at current prices. The full amount is typically paid in two installments — the ACV check upfront, then the "holdback" (depreciation) released after repairs are complete and documented.
- **Actual Cash Value (ACV):** The insurer deducts depreciation from the replacement cost. On a 15-year-old roof with a 20-year lifespan, you might receive only 25% of replacement cost — often not enough to cover a full replacement after your deductible.

If you're unsure which basis your policy uses, look for the words "replacement cost" on the Declarations page. ACV policies are less common on owner-occupied homes but appear frequently on rental properties and older policies that were never updated. Upgrading to RCV coverage typically adds $80–$200 per year to your premium — worth considering before next spring's storm season.

## Spotting Storm Chasers vs. Local Contractors

After any significant hail event in the KC metro, out-of-state roofing crews appear within 48 hours. Some are legitimate. Many are not. Red flags:

- They knocked on your door unsolicited within 24 hours of the storm and are offering a "free inspection" in exchange for signing a direction-of-payment form immediately.
- The contract lacks a Missouri business address or a Missouri contractor license number.
- They can't produce a Certificate of Insurance showing general liability coverage of at least $1 million and workers' compensation.
- The RSMo 429.012 lien disclosure is absent from the contract (see above).
- They ask you to pay the full deductible upfront before any work is scheduled.

A licensed local contractor will have a verifiable Missouri address, carry proper insurance, include the statutory lien disclosure, and won't pressure you to sign before your adjuster has even visited.

## Missouri Building Code Requirements for Roof Replacement

Kansas City operates under the 2018 International Residential Code (IRC) with local amendments. Key requirements your contractor must follow on a full replacement:

- **Underlayment:** IRC R905.2.7 requires a minimum of one layer of No. 15 felt (or equivalent synthetic) on slopes 4:12 and above. Slopes between 2:12 and 4:12 require two layers or a self-adhering polymer-modified bitumen sheet.
- **Drip edge:** Required at both eaves and rakes (IRC R905.2.8.5). The drip edge must be installed beneath the underlayment at the eaves and over the underlayment at the rakes.
- **Valley flashing:** Open metal valleys must be a minimum 24-gauge galvanized steel or equivalent.
- **Permit:** Kansas City, MO requires a building permit for full roof replacement. Overland Park (Johnson County, KS side of the metro) also requires permits for replacement. A contractor who tells you no permit is needed for a replacement is either wrong or cutting corners — either way, the liability falls on the homeowner if an uninspected roof is later discovered during a home sale.

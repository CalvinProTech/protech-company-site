# Roof Replacement Cost in St. Louis, MO (2026 Pricing Guide)

St. Louis roofs take a beating. Summers push past 95°F. Winters bring ice storms and freeze-thaw cycles that crack and lift shingles from November through March. And spring — tornado season — adds wind and hail on top of everything else. By the time most St. Louis homeowners decide to replace a roof, it's been leaking or failing for longer than they realize.

Here's what a full roof replacement actually costs in the St. Louis metro in 2026, broken down by material, home size, and the factors that move the price up or down.

## What St. Louis Homeowners Pay for a New Roof

For a typical 1,500–2,500 sq ft St. Louis home, expect to pay:

- **3-tab asphalt shingles**: $7,200–$10,500
- **Architectural (dimensional) shingles**: $9,000–$14,000
- **Impact-resistant (Class 4) shingles**: $11,500–$16,500
- **Metal roofing (standing seam)**: $15,000–$26,000
- **Cedar shake**: $18,000–$28,000

These ranges include tear-off of one existing layer, new underlayment, ice-and-water shield along eaves and valleys, drip edge, ridge cap, and flashing replacement. They do not include structural repairs to the deck or rafters — those are priced separately if found during tear-off.

Labor in the St. Louis metro (St. Louis City, St. Louis County, St. Charles County, Jefferson County) runs $2.00–$3.75 per square foot, slightly lower than Chicago but higher than rural Missouri. Materials account for roughly 40% of total project cost.

## The Freeze-Thaw Problem and Why Material Choice Matters

St. Louis averages 20–25 freeze-thaw cycles per winter — days when temperatures cross 32°F in both directions. Water infiltrates microcracks in shingles, freezes, expands, and widens those cracks. Standard 3-tab shingles are particularly vulnerable because they're thinner (a single mat layer) and have fewer granules per square foot.

If your current roof is 3-tab and it's older than 15 years, it has almost certainly been cycling through this damage for years. Replacing with architectural shingles adds a second fiberglass mat layer, which handles thermal movement better and typically carries a 30-year manufacturer warranty versus 25 years for 3-tab.

For homeowners who've had two or more hail claims in the last decade, Class 4 impact-resistant shingles are worth the added cost. Products like GAF Timberline ArmorShield II or Owens Corning Duration Flex carry a UL 2218 Class 4 rating, which qualifies most St. Louis-area homeowners for a 20–30% discount on homeowner's insurance premiums through insurers like State Farm and Shelter Insurance. That discount often recoups the cost difference within 5–7 years.

## How Roof Size and Pitch Affect the Final Number

Roofing is priced by the "square" — 100 sq ft of roof surface. A 2,000 sq ft home doesn't have a 20-square roof. The actual square count depends on pitch (steepness).

| Home Footprint | Low Pitch (4/12) | Moderate Pitch (6/12) | Steep Pitch (9/12+) |
|----------------|------------------|-----------------------|----------------------|
| 1,500 sq ft    | 18–20 squares    | 22–24 squares         | 26–30 squares        |
| 2,000 sq ft    | 24–26 squares    | 28–32 squares         | 34–40 squares        |
| 2,500 sq ft    | 30–34 squares    | 36–40 squares         | 42–50 squares        |

Steeper roofs cost more per square because they require safety equipment, slower installation, and more waste in cutting shingles to fit. A 9/12 pitch can add $1.50–$2.50 per square foot to labor alone.

Many St. Louis homes in Soulard, Tower Grove, and the older St. Louis County suburbs have complex rooflines — multiple valleys, dormers, and chimneys. Each penetration requires additional flashing work, adding $150–$600 per item depending on size and material.

## What Drives Costs Higher Than the Base Estimate

A few specific situations push St. Louis roof replacement costs above the ranges listed above:

**Multiple existing layers.** Missouri building code (per the 2018 International Residential Code as adopted by Missouri) allows a maximum of two roofing layers. If your home already has two layers, both must be torn off before new shingles can be installed. Double tear-off adds $500–$1,500 to a typical project.

**Decking damage.** Ice dams — common in St. Louis after the February ice storms that hit the region in 2021 and 2023 — can soak roof decking through damaged or missing ice-and-water shield. Rotted or delaminated OSB decking is replaced at $75–$120 per sheet (a 4×8 panel covering 32 sq ft). A heavily damaged deck can add $1,500–$3,500 to the total.

**Flat or low-slope sections.** Many ranch-style homes in Florissant, Mehlville, and Affton have low-slope porch or garage roofs that require a different waterproofing system — typically TPO membrane or modified bitumen — priced at $4.50–$8.50 per square foot installed.

**Chimney flashing.** St. Louis has a high percentage of homes with masonry chimneys. Step flashing plus counter flashing on a chimney runs $350–$700. If the chimney itself has deteriorated mortar (common in pre-1980 homes), tuckpointing is a separate masonry cost, not a roofing cost.

## Insurance Claims for St. Louis Roof Damage

Hail is the primary driver of insurance-covered roof replacements in Missouri. NOAA's Storm Events Database records an average of 18–24 significant hail events per year in the St. Louis metro, with the April–June window accounting for the majority.

If you're filing a claim after a hail or wind event, understand how your policy works before agreeing to anything:

**ACV vs. RCV policies.** ACV (Actual Cash Value) policies pay replacement cost minus depreciation. On a 15-year-old roof, that depreciation can be 40–60%, leaving you with a check that covers less than half the job. RCV (Replacement Cost Value) policies pay the full replacement cost, minus your deductible, after you complete the work.

**The supplement process.** Insurance adjusters frequently miss items during initial estimates — drip edge, ice-and-water shield upgrades, code-required changes. A licensed contractor can submit a supplemental claim for these items. This is standard practice, not fraud, and it's the most common way homeowners recover $800–$2,500 in underpaid claims.

**Missouri's 2% hail deductible.** Many policies issued after 2015 include a percentage-based deductible for hail rather than a flat dollar deductible. On a $200,000 home, 2% equals a $4,000 out-of-pocket cost — something many homeowners don't realize until the claim is filed.

## Financing a St. Louis Roof Replacement

If you're paying out of pocket, a $12,000–$16,000 roof replacement is a significant outlay. Several financing options are available:

**Home equity loan or HELOC.** If you have equity built up, this is usually the lowest-interest option — rates in the 7–9% range as of mid-2026. The interest may be tax-deductible if the funds are used for home improvement.

**Contractor financing.** Many reputable roofing contractors offer 12-month same-as-cash or longer-term installment plans through lenders like GreenSky or Mosaic. Rates vary widely (6.99–24.99% APR) depending on credit score, so read the terms carefully before signing.

**Missouri PACE financing.** Property Assessed Clean Energy programs are available in parts of Missouri and can finance energy-efficient roofing upgrades repaid through your property tax bill. Coverage and availability vary by municipality.

## Choosing a Contractor in St. Louis

Missouri doesn't require a statewide roofing contractor license, which makes contractor quality highly variable. Look for:

- **GAF Master Elite certification** — requires passing a background check, maintaining insurance, and demonstrating installation quality. Fewer than 2% of U.S. roofing contractors hold this designation.
- **Active general liability and workers' compensation insurance** — request certificates, not just verbal confirmation. An uninsured crew on your property creates personal liability exposure.
- **A written warranty on workmanship** — separate from the manufacturer's material warranty. A 10-year labor warranty is the industry standard among established contractors; 2-year warranties indicate a contractor who doesn't expect callbacks.
- **No door-to-door pressure after storms** — legitimate contractors don't chase storm events with same-day contract pressure. "Storm chasers" frequently do substandard work and may be gone before warranty issues arise.

Get at least three itemized bids. The lowest bid is often low because it excludes tear-off, ice-and-water shield, or drip edge — line items that cost money but don't show up on a simple per-square price.

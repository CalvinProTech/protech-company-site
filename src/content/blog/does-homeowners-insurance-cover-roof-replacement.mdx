# Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Roof Replacement? What You Need to Know in 2026

One of the most common questions homeowners ask after a storm is whether their insurance will pay for a new roof. The short answer: **yes, in most cases, homeowners insurance covers roof replacement when the damage is caused by a covered peril** like wind, hail, falling trees, or fire.

But the details matter. Understanding what your policy covers, how to file correctly, and what mistakes to avoid can be the difference between a fully covered replacement and a denied claim.

## What Homeowners Insurance Typically Covers

Standard homeowners insurance policies (HO-3) cover roof damage caused by:

- **Wind damage** — missing shingles, lifted flashing, exposed underlayment
- **Hail damage** — dented or cracked shingles, granule loss
- **Falling objects** — tree limbs, debris
- **Fire and lightning**
- **Weight of ice and snow** — structural damage from ice dams

If any of these events damaged your roof, your insurance company is typically obligated to pay for repairs or full replacement, minus your deductible.

## What Homeowners Insurance Does NOT Cover

Insurance generally does not cover:

- **Normal wear and tear** — a 25-year-old roof that's simply reached end of life
- **Neglected maintenance** — leaks that went unrepaired for months or years
- **Cosmetic damage** — dents that don't affect function (some policies exclude this)
- **Flood damage** — requires a separate flood insurance policy
- **Earthquakes** — requires separate coverage

The key distinction: **damage from a sudden event is covered; gradual deterioration is not.**

## The Age Factor: Does Roof Age Affect Coverage?

This is where many homeowners get confused. Your roof's age does affect how much your insurance pays, but it usually doesn't disqualify you from coverage entirely.

**Replacement Cost Value (RCV):** Your policy pays the full cost to replace the roof with similar materials, regardless of the roof's age. This is the better policy type.

**Actual Cash Value (ACV):** Your policy pays the depreciated value of the roof. A 15-year-old roof with a 25-year lifespan might only receive 40% of the replacement cost. The rest comes out of pocket.

Most modern policies use RCV for roofs under 10-15 years old and may switch to ACV for older roofs. Check your declarations page or call your agent to confirm which type you have.

## How to File a Roof Insurance Claim: Step by Step

### 1. Document the Damage Immediately

After a storm, take photos and videos of your roof from the ground. Look for:
- Missing or displaced shingles
- Dented gutters or downspouts (indicates hail)
- Debris on the roof
- Water stains on interior ceilings

### 2. Get a Professional Roof Inspection

A qualified roofing contractor can identify damage that isn't visible from the ground. At ProTech Roofing, we provide **free storm damage inspections** and detailed documentation that speaks the language insurance adjusters understand.

### 3. File Your Claim Promptly

Most policies require you to file within 1-2 years of the damage event, but sooner is better. Call your insurance company and provide:
- Date of the storm
- Description of the damage
- Photos and contractor inspection report

### 4. Be Present for the Adjuster Inspection

When the insurance adjuster visits, have your roofing contractor present. A knowledgeable contractor can point out damage the adjuster might miss and ensure nothing is overlooked.

### 5. Review the Settlement Carefully

Don't accept the first offer if it doesn't cover the full scope of work. Supplements — additional claims for items the adjuster missed — are common and legitimate. A good roofing contractor will file supplements on your behalf.

## Common Mistakes That Get Claims Denied

1. **Waiting too long to file** — evidence deteriorates and deadlines pass
2. **Not documenting damage** — photos are your best evidence
3. **Making temporary repairs without documenting first** — fix leaks to prevent further damage, but photograph everything before and after
4. **Accepting the first offer without review** — adjusters frequently undervalue claims
5. **Hiring a contractor who doesn't understand insurance** — you need someone who can write Xactimate estimates and file supplements

## What If Your Claim Is Denied?

A denial isn't always final. Common reasons for denial include:

- Damage classified as pre-existing or maintenance-related
- Missing documentation
- Policy exclusions you weren't aware of

You can appeal a denial by providing additional documentation, getting a second inspection, or hiring a public adjuster. Many denied claims are overturned with proper evidence.
